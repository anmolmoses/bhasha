# Three-Minute Demo

Bhasha is like Wispr Flow, but with translation: speak in any supported language and it types your message in the language you choose, inside any app. This script follows the rubric's recommended shape: 30 seconds of business context, 30 seconds of the manual workflow today, 2 minutes of live demo, closing on the metric.

## 0:00–0:30 — Business context (no tech)

> "My mother speaks and types Kannada comfortably. But her grandchild's school, her bank, and her building society all run on English WhatsApp messages. Every day a handful of them arrive that she can't confidently read or answer. She waits — sometimes hours — for me to translate, and some messages simply never get a reply. Bhasha's goal is simple: she answers those messages herself, in the hour they arrive."

## 0:30–1:00 — The manual workflow today

> "Here's what answering one message takes today: read the English message, not fully understand it, call your child or wait for them to come home, have them explain it, dictate a reply, have them type it. Or the do-it-yourself version: select, copy, leave WhatsApp, open a translator app, paste, translate, copy, switch back, paste, and hope nothing got lost. Ten steps, two apps, and it's still not in her own words. Most parents just wait instead."

## 1:00–3:00 — Live demo

All of this happens inside WhatsApp. Bhasha is only the floating bubble; the parent never leaves the conversation, and Bhasha never sends anything — the parent always presses Send.

**Beat 1 — Type Kannada, tap, get English (~25s)**

1. Open a WhatsApp chat. Tap the message field and type a short Kannada sentence.
2. Tap the Bhasha bubble.

> "One tap. Bhasha reads the field, translates it, and replaces it in place — and speaks the English aloud in her saved voice, at her saved pace, so she hears what she's about to send, not just sees it."

**Beat 2 — Undo (~15s)**

3. Point at the Undo pill that appeared after the replacement, and tap it.

> "If the result isn't what she meant, one tap on Undo brings her own words back. Nothing she typed is ever destroyed. Notice the messages Bhasha shows her are in Kannada — the whole experience speaks her language, including the errors."

4. Tap the bubble again to re-translate, ready to send.

**Beat 3 — Hold to speak a code-mixed reply (~40s)**

5. Press and hold the bubble. It turns red and shows "Listening…".
6. Speak a natural Kannada-English mix, e.g.: "ನಾನು Wednesday morning ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ. ಹತ್ತು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ." (I'll come Wednesday morning at ten.)
7. Release.

> "She doesn't pick a language — she just talks the way she actually talks, Kannada with English words mixed in. Bhasha detects it, translates it, and appends the English to the field. The day, the time — preserved. She reviews it and presses Send herself."

**Beat 4 — Memory survives a restart (~30s)**

8. Kill the Bhasha app, reopen it, and show Settings.

> "Her setup is not a session. Her voice, her speaking pace, her reply tone, and the names she's approved — like her grandchild's name spelled the way the family spells it — survive a restart. She sets Bhasha up once, and it stays hers."

9. Trigger one more grammar fix from the bubble to show the saved name spelled correctly.

## 2:50–3:00 — Close on the metric

> "The metric we care about: the share of English messages this parent answers herself within the hour. Today that's roughly one in five, on our own baseline observation. Every message you just saw was answered in under a minute, alone, inside WhatsApp. That's the path from waiting for your child to not needing to."

## Fallback

If the live demo drops (network, device, or API failure on stage):

> "The network just failed — which, notably, did not destroy her text; Bhasha never loses what's in the field. Here's a screen recording of the exact same flow captured earlier on this device."

Keep a pre-recorded run of Beats 1–4 on the demo phone. Present it explicitly as a recording, never as a live result.
